\section{Weighted information gain}

In the standard Gaussian process setting, the maximum information gain after $T$ observations is
\begin{equation*}
\Gamma_T
= \frac{1}{2}
\max_{x_1,\dots,x_T}
\sum_{t=1}^T
\log\!\left(
1+\frac{\sigma_{t-1}^2(x_t)}{\sigma^2}
\right),
\end{equation*}
where $\sigma_{t-1}^2(x_t)$ denotes the posterior variance before observing $x_t$.

Motivated by the preceding discussion, we introduce the weighted sequential objective
\begin{equation*}
\Gamma_T^w
= \frac{1}{2}
\sum_{t=1}^T
\log\!\left(
1+\frac{w(x_t)^2\sigma_{t-1}^2(x_t)}{\sigma^2}
\right).
\end{equation*}

The weights act externally on the marginal gains while leaving the Gaussian process posterior unchanged. The motivation is that if $w$ is concentrated on a sufficiently small subset of the domain, then $\Gamma_T^w$ may admit sharper bounds than the ordinary information gain.
For example, if
\begin{equation*}
w(x)=c\,\mathbf{1}_A(x),
\end{equation*}
then
$$
\Gamma_T^w
=
\frac12
\sum_{t:\,x_t\in A}
\log\!\left(
1+\frac{c^2\sigma_{t-1}^2(x_t)}{\sigma^2}
\right).
$$
which resembles a transductive information gain restricted to $A$.

The relevance of this weighted information gain is that it directly
controls the quantity optimized by a natural weighted uncertainty
sampling rule. In particular, bounding $\Gamma_T^w$ implies that the
average weighted posterior uncertainty decreases over time.

\begin{lemma}[Weighted uncertainty sampling]
Let the sampling rule be given by
$$
x_t \in \arg\max_{x\in\mathcal X} w(x)\sigma_{t-1}(x),
$$
where $w:\mathcal X\rightarrow\mathbb R_{\geq 0}$. Assume that
$w(x)^2\sigma_{t-1}^2(x)\leq C$ for all $x,t$. Then
$$
\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^T 
w(x_t)\sigma_{t-1}(x_t)
\leq
\sqrt{
\frac{2C'}{T}\gamma_T^w
},
$$
where $C'$ is a constant depending only on $C$ and the noise variance,
and
$$
\gamma_T^w
=
\frac12
\max_{A\subset\mathcal X, |A|\leq T}
\sum_{t=1}^T
\log\left(
1+
\frac{w(x_t)^2\sigma_{t-1}^2(x_t)}
{\sigma^2}
\right)
$$
is the weighted information gain.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Cauchy--Schwarz,
$$
\sum_{t=1}^T w(x_t)\sigma_{t-1}(x_t)
\leq
\sqrt{
T
\sum_{t=1}^T
w(x_t)^2\sigma_{t-1}^2(x_t)
}.
$$

Let
$$
u_t=
\frac{
w(x_t)^2\sigma_{t-1}^2(x_t)
}{
\sigma^2
}.
$$

Since $u_t$ is bounded, there exists a constant $C'$ such that
$$
u_t\leq C'\log(1+u_t).
$$

Therefore,
$$
\sum_{t=1}^T
w(x_t)^2\sigma_{t-1}^2(x_t)
\leq
C'\sigma^2
\sum_{t=1}^T
\log\left(
1+
\frac{
w(x_t)^2\sigma_{t-1}^2(x_t)
}{
\sigma^2
}
\right).
$$

By the definition of $\gamma_T^w$,
$$
\sum_{t=1}^T
\log\left(
1+
\frac{
w(x_t)^2\sigma_{t-1}^2(x_t)
}{
\sigma^2
}
\right)
\leq
2\gamma_T^w .
$$

Combining both inequalities gives
$$
\sum_{t=1}^T w(x_t)\sigma_{t-1}(x_t)
\leq
\sqrt{
2TC'\sigma^2\gamma_T^w
}.
$$

Absorbing $\sigma^2$ into the constant and dividing by $T$ gives
$$
\frac{1}{T}
\sum_{t=1}^T w(x_t)\sigma_{t-1}(x_t)
\leq
\sqrt{
\frac{2C'}{T}\gamma_T^w
}.
$$
\end{proof}

\subsection{Why bounding weighted information gain matters}

The previous lemma shows that $\Gamma_T^w$ controls the convergence of
weighted uncertainty sampling. In particular, regions with large weights
require small posterior uncertainty, while regions with negligible weights
do not significantly contribute to the objective.

However, this result alone does not provide an improved rate: it reduces the
problem to obtaining sharp bounds on $\Gamma_T^w$. A naive comparison gives
only
$$
\Gamma_T^w
\leq
\Gamma_T\left(k,\frac{\sigma^2}{\|w\|_\infty^2}\right),
$$
which treats the entire domain as if it had the maximum possible weight.
This loses the localization effect introduced by $w$.

The main question is therefore whether the structure of the weighting
function can be exploited to obtain bounds depending only on the effective
weighted region rather than the full domain. The following sections study
when such improvements are possible and why existing information-gain
arguments cannot be applied directly.

\subsection{Loss of submodularity for weighted objective}

For ordinary information gain, the set function
\begin{equation*}
F(S)=I(f_S;y_S)
\end{equation*}
has marginal gains
\begin{equation*}
F(S\cup\{x\})-F(S)
=
\frac12
\log\!\left(
1+\frac{\sigma_S^2(x)}{\sigma^2}
\right),
\end{equation*}
which satisfy diminishing returns. This submodularity is what allows standard greedy approximation arguments.

The weighted objective does not inherit this structure. Writing formally
$$
F_w(x_1,\dots,x_T)
=
\frac12
\sum_{t=1}^T
\log\left(
1+
\frac{
w(x_t)^2\sigma_{t-1}^2(x_t)
}{
\sigma^2
}
\right),
$$
the contribution of a point depends both on its weight and on its position in the sequence through the posterior variance. As a result, the objective is order-dependent and thus cannot be expressed as a submodular set function.This prevents the direct application of the standard TAL framework.
